# 04 — Content Rules

These are rules about *what* goes inside each element, not where it sits
on the page.

- **Action** is written in third person, present tense, and describes only
  what a camera could see or a microphone could hear — no interior
  thoughts, no backstory exposition dressed as action. Paragraphs over 4
  lines are a *style* note, never a format error: some writers pace long
  action deliberately.
- **Character names** are always ALL CAPS, with no exceptions, including
  in dialogue-heavy comedy where a writer might be tempted to lowercase a
  running gag name.
- **Dialogue** never carries quotation marks — the indentation and the
  character cue above it already mark it as speech.
- **Parentheticals** are for a brief physical beat tied to the line
  (`(picking up the phone)`, `(to Dana)`), never for tone/emotion
  ("wrylies" like `(angrily)`, `(sarcastically)`) — those are considered
  a style anti-pattern because they direct the actor's performance
  instead of describing an event.
- **Extensions** — `(V.O.)`, `(O.S.)`, `(CONT'D)` — attach directly to the
  character name, not to the dialogue or a parenthetical.
- **Transitions** are conventionally `FADE IN:`, `FADE OUT.`, `CUT TO:`,
  `SMASH CUT TO:`, `CUT TO BLACK.` The trailing `TO:` is a convention, not
  a hard requirement — flag its absence at `style` severity only.
- **FREEZE FRAME** takes no trailing colon and is never followed by more
  described action — it functions as a full stop on the visual.
- **Emphasis** (bold/italic) is discouraged in spec (selling/reading)
  scripts but explicitly allowed in production documents, where it's
  used to flag revisions or draw a department's attention.

## Why this rule exists

Third-person present-tense action mirrors how the audience will actually
experience the finished film — as a sequence of present-moment images —
and keeps the page a blueprint for filming rather than a piece of prose.
Banning quotation marks and wrylies keeps the page lean: the format trusts
actors and directors to find the performance, and reserves the page for
what physically happens.
